title: "Ramayanas"

I walk into a bookstore and Ramayanas accost me.

I was 4 I think, when my grandfather told me the story of Rama. _The_ story, I
assumed. I loved the man to no end. He also told me stories from the Vishnu
Purana, especially the story of Gajendra Moksham. I think the image of Vishnu
coming down from Vaikuntha astride Suparna to save an elephant from a crocodile
was a vivid image for a child of four.

I believed that was it, Rama went to the forest to obey his father, and Sita was
taken away by Ravana and the Vanaras, especially Hanumantha, helped Rama get her
back after a great battle.

Ravana had amruth in his gut, and only Rama could defeat him.

That was _my_ Rama, the Rama that my grandfather left me with.

I suppose it was both fuelled by watching the Telugu _Sampoorna Ramayana_ film
and foggy memories of the man.

I didn't think much of it to tell you the truth. I wasn't obsessed with the
Ramayana for most of my life.

I also read the Bible far more times than I heard the Ramayana. I watched _Super
Book_, an anime version of Bible stories. It was cutesy, and I could narrate
stories from the Old Testament whenever an adult asked me to. I was surrounded
by Christians, you see.

The Ramayana lived rent free in my head. Perhaps Kubera should hear that. He
alone charges interest from a god.

I watched the Ramayana anime when it aired on Cartoon Network. I watched it in
English, predominantly. I loved it, I wished my grandfather could have lived to
see it. I remember him, dying of cancer, but still waking up to watch the old
Mahabharata and Ramayana TV shows.

It was a ritual for them, I suppose. As is reading verses from the Ramayana to
learn them by rote in my family. My cousins spend more time reading other texts,
not for the stories, just for the "benefit".

I'm sure there's something to be said about a non-vegetarian brahmin atheist
being obsessed by the story of Rama.

I didn't even enjoy Rajaji's Ramayana as a kid. It was boring. His Rama seemed
boring. I wish I could have read Kamala Subramaniam growing up.

At this point in my life, I'm known in certain circles for being obsessed by the
Ramayana. I am not sure what to tell you. At one point, I made a promise to my
_guru_. I told him I'd write again, and I told him that inside of me, the
Ramakathas are bubbling up. They are restless. They accost me at bookstores. He
asked met to wait. He said that the Ramakatha has a way of forcing us to write
about them when the time is write.

What am I supposed to say? That I've gotten a divine sign that I need to read
all of them? If I believed in that, my life would have been much easier. It
definitely would not have been this confusing.

I spend my days wondering about a found family, and about dying alone. I don't
fit into this family of mine, I cannot stand most of my cousins. I have never
ever fit in.

I've read more Ramakathas than most people you will meet, and I'm sure I say the
word Rama at least 30 times a day, while doing other things. It's become a
catch-all phrase. If my bones ache, I say Rama. If I struggle to get up in the
morning, I say Rama. At first, I was mimicking my father, who says Dasarathi
when he sneezes. Now, I'm not sure what I'm doing.

What _is_ Rama to me? People have asked me if I liked the name Rama for a child.
I don't really. Naming your son Rama in today's India is like naming your son
Jesus, almost. Maybe worse. I don't like that name very much. Raghava is a nice
name. But I don't recommend those names much. My niece is named Sita. It was the
only name my sister wanted for a daughter, and I love that name a lot. I was
reading Camille Buicke's The Rama Story, and in it, he writes that there were
multiple mentions of a Sita in the Rig Veda, but barely any mention of Rama. He
wrote that the Sita that was mentioned there was portrayed as a minor goddess of
agriculture. I wonder what goddess we coopted into this myth.

I began my strange obsession around the time I read Bibek Debroy's translation
of the Valmiki Ramayana. In his preface, he wrote of the Dasaratha Jakata, and
in hunting for it, I discovered Paula Richman's works and A.K. Ramanujan's essay
_Three Hundred Ramayanas_. I began buying any obscure work on the Ramayana I
could find. I'd read Ashok Banker's take as a teenager and enjoyed it, but I'd
since given away my copies of that. I didn't really want modern takes on it, I
was looking for different versions.

Why though? I'm not sure. Today, I cannot walk into a bookshop without being
accosted by a Ramayana I do not own. Three years ago, I had a shelf in an Ikea
Billy. Today, it's an entire Ikea Billy. An entire book case full of Ramakathas
or works on it.

I cannot run from the Ramayana. I'm not sure why it calls me so. All I can do is
listen, and speak the name while I go about my life. If it is a sin that someone
like me who loathes caste, ritual, religion and belief is spending this much
time pondering about the Ramakatha, so be it. My sin, perhaps, is the sin of
Saul. I only have one ear, and I lend it to the bards before me as they sing of
Rama of the Bow.
